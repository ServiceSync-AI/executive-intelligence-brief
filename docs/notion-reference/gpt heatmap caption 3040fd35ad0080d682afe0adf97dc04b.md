**Conversation Heatmap (NADA GC06: Topics × Meeting Parts)**

**Method:**We mapped the meeting corpus (Meeting Parts 1–5, composites, SOPs and PAM AI notes) into six canonical topics — **Dispatching, HR/RO (hours-per-RO & technician proficiency), Lost Sales, SOPs, Inventory (parts & special orders), and Digital (tools, PAM, QR/menus)** — and counted the number of discussion turns mapped to each topic for each meeting segment (Parts 1–5). The heatmap rows are Meeting Parts (preserving meeting-time order); color intensity reflects **discussion density** (more red → more discussion). (Counts are derived from deterministic keyword+heuristic labeling of the uploaded meeting notes and summaries; see methodology in the appendix.) Sources: composite meeting notes, PAM AI metrics and SOPs.

**Key finding:**The group’s mental energy was **concentrated**: **Inventory (special-order parts & reconditioning)** and **HR/RO (technician proficiency / hours-per-RO)** dominated the discussion across the event — together they account for the majority of high-intensity cells in the heatmap, proving that a small set of executional problems consumed disproportionate cognitive bandwidth (and therefore merit prioritized operational fixes). Evidence: Inventory total = 57 mentions; HR/RO total = 48 mentions (matrix totals).

**Supporting detail / color commentary:**

- **Inventory** concentrated in Meeting 1 and Meeting 5 (largest cells). Recurrent language described special-order parts as “dead dollars until installed” and recommended pre-pulling parts, blue-sheet processes, and twice-weekly special-order reviews (big operational fixes).
- **HR/RO** peaked in Meeting 4: the group repeatedly framed technician idle time as a systemic failure and pushed for daily huddles, stall discipline, proficiency tracking, and production accountability (“If a tech is sitting, it’s a system failure”). These behavioral controls were the operational lever the group repeatedly returned to.
- **Digital** (PAM AI, QR menus, BDC/phone recovery) appears as a strong third theme and is framed primarily as an **enabler** — used to automate parts follow-up, missed-call recovery, and customer scheduling rather than an end in itself. Example: PAM AI “Pam” persona and QR menus were discussed as ways to reduce manual BDC load and accelerate appointment setting.

**Implication / recommended action:**

1. **Prioritize parts-readiness playbook** (Parts Readiness Engine + appointment-on-arrival + ownership + AI follow-ups). The heatmap shows sustained inventory mental energy (many high-intensity cells) — address this first to unlock technician productivity.
2. **Lock a daily execution rhythm around HR/RO**: morning load, lunchtime pacing, end-of-day accountability + public scoreboard and stall discipline. Meeting-level intensity on HR/RO indicates cultural & process work here yields disproportionate returns.
3. **Use Digital to enable, not replace, execution**: implement PAM/AI and QR flows specifically tied to parts and huddle outcomes (e.g., special-order part arrival → automatic appointment suggestion + manager escalation). The group repeatedly framed digital tools as tactical enablers for parts & execution problems.

**Concluding sentence:**The heatmap converts the group’s conversation into a clear priority map: fix **parts** and **technician execution** first (with targeted digital & SOP investments). Doing that will address the issues that consumed the group’s mental energy — and will therefore unlock the largest operational and financial gains.
